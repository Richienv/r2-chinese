# Indonesian (Gen Z) style guide

The goal is that a learner reads a meaning and thinks "oh, jadi ini artinya itu", then can build their own
sentences. Clarity first, vibe second.

## Voice
- Santai, like a friend explaining over chat. Address the learner as **kamu**, include with **kita**.
- Light particles where they help: *nih, sih, deh, dong, kok, tuh, ya, yuk, banget, udah, gak, lagi*.
  At most one or two per sentence. No wall of slang.
- Write words out ("yang", "dengan", "juga"). Do **not** use text-speak abbreviations (yg, dgn, bgt, jg, utk, sm).
- Do not force English code-mixing. Keep an English word only when Indonesians really say it that way
  (*email, online, bos, WiFi*).
- No emoji unless the English has one.
- UI labels are short verbs: *Lanjut, Cek, Coba lagi, Selesai, Dengerin, Tulis, Simpan*.

## Accuracy beats vibe
- A **gloss** (a word's meaning) is the standard Indonesian equivalent, not slang. Short: 1 to 5 words.
  Keep the senses the English lists, in the same order, separated the same way (`;` or `,`).
  Keep what the English says about usage (formal, casual, polite, measure word, etc.).
- An **explanation or tip** can be casual, but every fact must survive: what the word does, where it goes in the
  sentence, what it contrasts with, the examples' meaning.
- Translate the **meaning** of example sentences naturally, the way an Indonesian would say it.
- Never change Chinese characters, pinyin, numbers, names of people/places in Hanzi, list numbering (①, (1), 1.),
  blanks (＿＿＿), or the bracketed markers. Copy them exactly.
- If the English is a placeholder like `{n}`, keep it exactly, including braces.
- Do not add facts the English does not have. Do not drop any.
